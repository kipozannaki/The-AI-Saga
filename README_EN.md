## 📖 About This Book

*The AI Saga* takes the eighty-plus years of artificial intelligence as its main storyline, with people, events, and technical breakthroughs at the heart of the narrative — weaving together historical threads that were originally scattered across papers, textbooks, news reports, and technical references.

The book sets no complex formulas as its reading threshold. Instead, it keeps asking one simple question:

> **The AI we take for granted today — how exactly did it grow, step by step, into what it is?**

From the neuron model and the Turing Test, to the Perceptron and the first AI winter; from expert systems, convolutional neural networks, backpropagation, and deep learning, to ImageNet, AlexNet, AlphaGo, Transformer, GPT, BERT, and ChatGPT; and on to Agents, reasoning models, Skills, long-horizon autonomous execution, world models, embodied intelligence, and multi-agent systems — the technologies keep changing, but what runs through the whole story are the people who never left during those long "winters."

What this book tries to record is not merely how technology advanced, but **how humanity, step by step, came to re-understand "intelligence" itself**.

## 🧭 Table of Contents

### Prologue
**The Ember Still Burns**

Why tell AI as a story? The answer is simple: technology itself is cold, but the people who make it are warm.

### Chapter 1: A Poor Boy in Exile Peers at the Heavens; A Child Prodigy's Debate Stuns the Scholars
**— The Neuron Model and the Turing Test**

From McCulloch and Pitts to Turing and the Dartmouth Conference, artificial intelligence gained, for the first time, a foundation that could be computed, discussed, and named.

### Chapter 2: The Press Cheers the Electronic Brain; Minsky's Single Paper Seals Its Fate
**— The Perceptron Fever and the First AI Winter**

Rosenblatt, the Perceptron, and the historical turning point that plummeted from euphoria into the first AI winter.

### Chapter 3: Expert Systems Rule the World; Knowledge Engineering Turns Out a Dream
**— Symbolicism, Expert Systems, and the Second AI Winter**

From DENDRAL and MYCIN to XCON, the symbolic school once blazed with confidence, only to crash into the realities of knowledge acquisition, rule maintenance, and system fragility.

### Chapter 4: Cats Open the Eyes of a New Era; Fukushima Builds the Divine Machine and Passes the Torch
**— The Birth and Quiet Years of Convolutional Neural Networks**

From Hubel and Wiesel's research on the visual system, to Fukushima Kunihiko, backpropagation, and LeNet — the ember of neural networks was rekindled in the depths of winter.

### Chapter 5: Three Swordsmen Keep a Lonely Lamp in the Dark; One Ember Buried Deep Awaits Spring
**— The Long Vigil Before Deep Learning Broke Ground**

Hinton, LeCun, Bengio, and other researchers pressed on through the coldest, quietest years of neural networks, waiting for algorithms, data, and computing power to finally converge.

### Chapter 6: A Million Images Fed to the Nerves; Brute Force Stuns the Champions
**— ImageNet, GPUs, and AlexNet**

Large-scale datasets, GPU parallel computing, and deep neural networks finally met in the same era — 2012 became the pivotal moment when deep learning truly broke ground.

### Chapter 7: Google Pays a Fortune for the Ember; AlphaGo Moves First and Decides All
**— The Full Eruption of Deep Learning and AI Going Mainstream**

From the industrialization of deep learning to the match between AlphaGo and Lee Sedol, machines stepped out of the laboratory and into the view of ordinary people worldwide for the first time, in the most striking way possible.

### Chapter 8: One Web of Attention, Ten Thousand Tongues Made One
**— The Birth of Transformer and the Language Revolution**

From RNN and LSTM to Attention and Transformer, "attention" redefined how machines process language and information, laying the most crucial technical skeleton for the era of large models to come.

### Chapter 9: GPT and BERT, Two Heroes Stand Side by Side; The Pre-training Paradigm Unifies the Realm
**— Prelude to the Era of Large Models**

From pre-training ideas and BERT to GPT and GPT-3, AI began moving from "one task, one model" toward a more general foundation-model paradigm — scale became the new road.

### Chapter 10: ChatGPT Stuns the World Overnight; An AI Tide Sweeps Five Continents in Two Months
**— Large Models Reach Hundreds of Millions of Users**

When Transformer, large-scale training, RLHF, and productization finally converged into a simple chat box, AI stepped into the daily lives of ordinary people for the very first time.

### Chapter 11: Code Flows Through Harvard at Midnight; The Agent Is Born and the World Trembles
**— The Birth and Evolution of AI Agents**

From tool calling and ReAct to LangChain, BabyAGI, AutoGPT, and multi-agent systems, AI began its journey from "able to answer" to "able to act."

### Chapter 12: A Quant Prodigy Breaks Down the Walls; Open-Source R1 Shakes the World at Low Cost
**— The Arrival of the Reasoning-Model Era and the Turn Ahead**

From o1 to DeepSeek-R1, AI began shifting from "answer immediately" to "take time to think" — reasoning became the new technological leap, and further opened the door to agentic thinking.

### Chapter 13: Skill Sets the Standard and Opens a New Game; OpenClaw Stuns the World Overnight
**— From Tool Calling to Long-Horizon Autonomous Execution**

Skills, Memory, OpenClaw, Hermes, and long-horizon agents gave AI a more standardized, reusable, and sustainable working capability — moving from one-shot responses toward long-term execution.

### Chapter 14: The World Is No Longer Just Words; Machines Finally Step into Reality
**— World Models, Embodied Intelligence, Multi-Agent Systems, and AI Safety**

As AI moves from the world of text into the physical world, it must learn not only to think and act, but also to understand its environment, predict consequences, and collaborate continuously — while facing the safety and governance questions that follow.

### Epilogue
**Do Machines Truly Have Intelligence? What Exactly Have We Built?**

Eighty-three years of technical history finally return to the original question: do machines have intelligence? And that question leads us back to another: what, after all, is a human being?

### Afterword
**The Ember Still Burns**

The eighty-three-year story has not truly ended. What we call "the frontier" today will, one day, become the starting point in the eyes of those who come after.

## 🔥 What This Book Really Wants to Tell You Is More Than AI

Strip away all the technical jargon, and the book can still be summarized as a chain of questions:

```text
Can machines compute?
        ↓
Can machines learn?
        ↓
Can machines see?
        ↓
Can machines understand language?
        ↓
Can machines converse with humans?
        ↓
Can machines reason?
        ↓
Can machines act?
        ↓
Can machines work long-term and remember experience?
        ↓
Can machines understand the real world?
        ↓
And so — do machines really count as "intelligence"?
```

This is the one question the book never truly answers from beginning to end.

## ✨ How This Book Is Written

This is not a machine-learning course that strictly follows textbook conventions, nor a survey of papers.

It is closer to a "romance of science":

**Turn technology back into people, papers back into stories, and every failure back into a long exploration.**

A basic principle of the writing:

> **Never just tell readers "what happened later" — make them understand "why the people of that time walked down that path."**

Hence a recurring narrative structure throughout the book:

```text
A question
   ↓
A person
   ↓
A bold idea
   ↓
A breakthrough
   ↓
A misjudgment / a failure
   ↓
A long winter
   ↓
Another group of people picks up the ember
   ↓
A new technological revolution
```

## 🌍 Why Open Source?

Because the development of artificial intelligence has always been a relay of knowledge across borders, disciplines, and eras.

By publishing this book on GitHub, I hope it will not remain a "finished book," but become an open project that can be continuously revised, corrected, supplemented, and discussed.

Future additions may include:

- Markdown / DOCX / PDF reading editions
- An AI history timeline
- A genealogy of AI pioneers
- An index of key papers and primary sources
- Diagrams of technological evolution
- A Chinese–English glossary
- Errata and revision records
- Open resources related to AI history

If you find factual errors in the book — in dates, personal histories, or technical descriptions — Issues and Pull Requests are always welcome.

**This book is about a relay across generations; therefore it should also allow those who come after to keep revising it.**

## 🔭 Finally

In 1943, humanity drew the outline of a neuron with mathematics for the first time.

In 1950, Turing asked: **"Can machines think?"**

In 2012, deep learning began to truly change how machines see the world.

In 2017, Transformer rewrote the paradigm of how machines process language and information.

In 2022, ChatGPT let hundreds of millions of people truly converse with AI for the first time.

In 2024–2025, reasoning models taught AI to "take time to think."

In 2026, AI began trying to work long-term, call tools, understand the physical world, and collaborate with other agents.

Yet here the story stands, and we still don't know whether the original question has an answer.

Perhaps eighty years from now, people will look back at today and smile, the way we smile looking back at the Perceptron.

Perhaps they won't.

But no matter what —

> **Every today was once a yesterday that no one believed in.**
>
> **The ember still burns.**
